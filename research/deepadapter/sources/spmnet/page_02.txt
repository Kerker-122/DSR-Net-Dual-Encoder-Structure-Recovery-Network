Anything Model 2 (SAM2) [6] have also been introduced into downstream segmentation tasks due
to their strong general representation capabilities. In 2024, Yan et al. proposed MAS-SAM [14],
which employs adapters, a Hypermap Extraction Module (HEM), and a Progressive Prediction
Decoder (PPD) to adapt SAM to marine scenes. The Segment Anything Model 2 UNet (SAM2-
UNet) [5] proposed by Xiong et al. further exploits the representation capability of the SAM2
encoder and achieves promising performance across various segmentation tasks. However, feature
enhancement or conventional cross-level fusion alone remains insufficient to address the two
challenges above in highly camouflaged underwater scenes. On the one hand, shallow features
contain rich spatial details but also preserve substantial background textures that resemble the target.
Directly fusing these features may further interfere with already weakened target semantics. On the
other hand, existing data-driven decoding processes mainly recover object contours from visual
features and lack explicit constraints on object shape. Consequently, discontinuous or incomplete
predictions still occur around low-contrast boundaries and slender structures such as tentacles.

To address these problems, we propose SPMNet, a shape-prior guided network for camouflaged
marine animal segmentation. Built upon SAM2, SPMNet introduces a Progressive SAG-CCS
Decoder (PSD) as its core decoding architecture. Inspired by the Convex Combination Star Shape
(CCS) prior proposed by Zhao et al. [22], we integrate an Iterative CCS Refinement (ICCS)
mechanism into PSD, enabling progressive decoding under explicit shape constraints. Its smooth
shape field provides explicit shape constraints for predicted contours, improving the recovery of
ambiguous boundaries and slender structures such as tentacles. However, in complex camouflage
scenarios, high foreground-background  similarity weakens  target semantics and  introduces
substantial background interference into shallow features. Such unreliable features can further limit
the effectiveness of ICCS during shape-guided decoding. To address this issue, we further introduce
a Spatial Attention Gate (SAG) into PSD. SAG selectively enhances target-related spatial responses
and suppresses background interference during cross-level fusion, providing more reliable features
for subsequent shape-prior guided decoding. During training, a Hybrid Shape-Aware Loss combines
region-level classification supervision with shape-direction consistency to jointly optimize region
prediction and boundary structure. In addition, the frozen SAM2 Hiera backbone is equipped with
MonaAdapter [7], a Context-aware Feature Enhancement Module (CFEM), and a Receptive Field
Block (RFB) [8] for lightweight adaptation and contextual enhancement. Through these designs,
SPMNet takes the CCS shape prior as the core of progressive decoding and employs SAG to
improve its effectiveness under complex camouflage, thereby better addressing semantic weakening
and boundary ambiguity in marine animal segmentation.

We name the improved model the Shape-Prior Guided Progressive Decoding for Camouflaged
Marine Animal Segmentation (SPMNet). The main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows:

1. We propose a Progressive SAG-CCS Decoder (PSD) that integrates an Iterative CCS Refinement
(ICCS) mechanism into progressive decoding. Explicit shape constraints are introduced to improve
the recovery of ambiguous boundaries and slender structures. In addition, a Spatial Attention Gate
(SAG)  is incorporated to suppress feature interference caused by camouflaged backgrounds,
providing reliable representations for shape-prior guided decoding.
