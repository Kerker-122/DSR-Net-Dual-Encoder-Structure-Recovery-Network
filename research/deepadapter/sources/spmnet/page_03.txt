2. Related Work

2.1 Marine Animal Segmentation (MAS)

Owing to the progress of deep neural networks and the widespread availability of extensive labeled
data, object segmentation based on deep learning has achieved remarkable advancements in recent
decades. Tailored to different visual tasks, researchers have proposed various segmentation methods.
For example, Olaf Ronneberger et al. proposed U-Net, which combines symmetric contracting and
expanding paths with skip connections to achieve high-precision segmentation on limited medical
image data [9]. He et al. added a mask branch to the Faster R-CNN architecture, enabling the
detection and high-quality mask segmentation of multi-instance targets within images [10]. Xie et
al. developed SegFormer [11], which combines hierarchical Transformer representations with a
lightweight decoding head for semantic segmentation. These features are subsequently integrated
with a lightweight MLP-based decoding head to achieve efficient semantic segmentation while
maintaining competitive accuracy.

However, compared with general and medical image segmentation, research on marine animal
segmentation remains relatively limited and faces several unique challenges. Underwater imaging
conditions, including illumination variations, suspended particles, and water turbidity, significantly
degrade image quality. Furthermore, during long-term evolution, many marine organisms have
developed body colors and textures that are highly similar to their surrounding environments,
enabling effective visual camouflage. This strong integration with the background weakens target
semantic representations and increases the difficulty of accurate segmentation.

been proposed in recent years. Li et al. developed ECDNet [12], which employs cascaded decoding
to progressively refine marine animal predictions. Chen et al. proposed the WaterSNet framework,

robust segmentation of camouflaged marine animals [13]. Yan et al. (2024) presented the MAS-
