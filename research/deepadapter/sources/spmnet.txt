    SPMNet: Shape-Prior Guided Progressive Decoding

     for Camouflaged Marine Animal Segmentation


Abstract: Complex underwater backgrounds and biological camouflage often weaken target
semantics, while low contrast and non-rigid morphologies lead to ambiguous boundaries and
incomplete structures. To address these issues, we propose SPMNet, a shape-prior guided network
for marine animal segmentation. Built upon SAM2, SPMNet introduces a Progressive SAG-CCS
Decoder (PSD) for progressive semantic and structural reconstruction. Within PSD, a Spatial
Attention Gate (SAG) suppresses camouflage-induced background responses during cross-level
feature fusion, providing more reliable decoding features. An Iterative CCS Refinement (ICCS)
mechanism based on the Convex Combination Star Shape (CCS) prior then imposes explicit
geometric constraints to improve boundary continuity and structural completeness. We further
design a Hybrid Shape-Aware Loss (HSAL), which combines pixel-level region supervision with
gradient-direction consistency. A frozen SAM2 Hiera backbone with lightweight adaptation and
contextual enhancement modules is used for underwater feature representation. Experiments on
MAS3K, RMAS, UFO120, and RUWI demonstrate competitive performance. Notably, on MAS3K,
outperforming state-of-the-art models by 1.6% in 𝑚𝐼𝑜𝑈, 1.3% in 𝐹𝛽𝜔,and 0.6% in 𝑚𝐸𝜑, with a
0.1% reduction in MAE.

Keywords: Marine Animal Segmentation, Camouflaged Object Segmentation, Shape  Prior,
Progressive Decoding

1．Introduction

Marine Animal Segmentation (MAS)  is an important vision task for underwater ecological
monitoring, biodiversity investigation, and marine resource management. Unlike conventional
object segmentation in natural scenes, MAS is affected by underwater imaging factors such as light
absorption, scattering, water turbidity, and non-uniform illumination [1–3]. Biological camouflage
introduces an additional challenge. Many marine animals resemble surrounding coral, rocks, and
sand through similar colors, textures, or morphological patterns. As a result, target features are easily
disturbed by background textures, reducing their discriminability in the feature space. We refer to
this phenomenon as semantic weakening. Meanwhile, low-contrast underwater imaging and
complex non-rigid morphologies make object contours difficult to distinguish. This problem is
particularly severe for tentacles, fins, and other slender appendages, where segmentation results
often contain unclear contours, local discontinuities, or missing structures. We refer to this problem
as boundary ambiguity. Therefore, maintaining  discriminative  target semantics  in complex
backgrounds while recovering continuous and complete boundaries remains a key challenge for
camouflaged marine animal segmentation.

In recent years, several methods have been developed for marine animal segmentation in complex
underwater environments. Li et al. proposed the Enhanced Cascade Decoder Network (ECDNet)
[12], which progressively refines marine animal segmentation through a cascaded decoding
mechanism. Chen et al. later developed WaterSNet [13], using Random Style Adaptation (RSA) and
feature fusion to alleviate interference caused by complex underwater degradation. With the

=== PDF PAGE 2 ===
development of vision foundation models, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) and Segment
Anything Model 2 (SAM2) [6] have also been introduced into downstream segmentation tasks due
to their strong general representation capabilities. In 2024, Yan et al. proposed MAS-SAM [14],
which employs adapters, a Hypermap Extraction Module (HEM), and a Progressive Prediction
Decoder (PPD) to adapt SAM to marine scenes. The Segment Anything Model 2 UNet (SAM2-
UNet) [5] proposed by Xiong et al. further exploits the representation capability of the SAM2
encoder and achieves promising performance across various segmentation tasks. However, feature
enhancement or conventional cross-level fusion alone remains insufficient to address the two
challenges above in highly camouflaged underwater scenes. On the one hand, shallow features
contain rich spatial details but also preserve substantial background textures that resemble the target.
Directly fusing these features may further interfere with already weakened target semantics. On the
other hand, existing data-driven decoding processes mainly recover object contours from visual
features and lack explicit constraints on object shape. Consequently, discontinuous or incomplete
predictions still occur around low-contrast boundaries and slender structures such as tentacles.

To address these problems, we propose SPMNet, a shape-prior guided network for camouflaged
marine animal segmentation. Built upon SAM2, SPMNet introduces a Progressive SAG-CCS
Decoder (PSD) as its core decoding architecture. Inspired by the Convex Combination Star Shape
(CCS) prior proposed by Zhao et al. [22], we integrate an Iterative CCS Refinement (ICCS)
mechanism into PSD, enabling progressive decoding under explicit shape constraints. Its smooth
shape field provides explicit shape constraints for predicted contours, improving the recovery of
ambiguous boundaries and slender structures such as tentacles. However, in complex camouflage
scenarios, high foreground-background  similarity weakens  target semantics and  introduces
substantial background interference into shallow features. Such unreliable features can further limit
the effectiveness of ICCS during shape-guided decoding. To address this issue, we further introduce
a Spatial Attention Gate (SAG) into PSD. SAG selectively enhances target-related spatial responses
and suppresses background interference during cross-level fusion, providing more reliable features
for subsequent shape-prior guided decoding. During training, a Hybrid Shape-Aware Loss combines
region-level classification supervision with shape-direction consistency to jointly optimize region
prediction and boundary structure. In addition, the frozen SAM2 Hiera backbone is equipped with
MonaAdapter [7], a Context-aware Feature Enhancement Module (CFEM), and a Receptive Field
Block (RFB) [8] for lightweight adaptation and contextual enhancement. Through these designs,
SPMNet takes the CCS shape prior as the core of progressive decoding and employs SAG to
improve its effectiveness under complex camouflage, thereby better addressing semantic weakening
and boundary ambiguity in marine animal segmentation.

We name the improved model the Shape-Prior Guided Progressive Decoding for Camouflaged
Marine Animal Segmentation (SPMNet). The main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows:

1. We propose a Progressive SAG-CCS Decoder (PSD) that integrates an Iterative CCS Refinement
(ICCS) mechanism into progressive decoding. Explicit shape constraints are introduced to improve
the recovery of ambiguous boundaries and slender structures. In addition, a Spatial Attention Gate
(SAG)  is incorporated to suppress feature interference caused by camouflaged backgrounds,
providing reliable representations for shape-prior guided decoding.

2. We construct SPMNet, a SAM2-based network for camouflaged marine animal segmentation.
MonaAdapter is incorporated into the frozen SAM2 Hiera backbone for lightweight adaptation to
underwater scenes. Meanwhile, the Context-aware Feature Enhancement Module (CFEM) and
Receptive Field Block (RFB) are used to enhance multi-scale contextual information and provide
more discriminative features for the subsequent PSD.

3. We design a Hybrid Shape-Aware Loss that combines pixel-level region supervision with shape-

structure  of  the  target. Experiments on  multiple marine animal segmentation benchmarks
demonstrate the competitive performance of SPMNet.

2. Related Work

2.1 Marine Animal Segmentation (MAS)

Owing to the progress of deep neural networks and the widespread availability of extensive labeled
data, object segmentation based on deep learning has achieved remarkable advancements in recent
decades. Tailored to different visual tasks, researchers have proposed various segmentation methods.
For example, Olaf Ronneberger et al. proposed U-Net, which combines symmetric contracting and
expanding paths with skip connections to achieve high-precision segmentation on limited medical
image data [9]. He et al. added a mask branch to the Faster R-CNN architecture, enabling the
detection and high-quality mask segmentation of multi-instance targets within images [10]. Xie et
al. developed SegFormer [11], which combines hierarchical Transformer representations with a
lightweight decoding head for semantic segmentation. These features are subsequently integrated
with a lightweight MLP-based decoding head to achieve efficient semantic segmentation while
maintaining competitive accuracy.

However, compared with general and medical image segmentation, research on marine animal
segmentation remains relatively limited and faces several unique challenges. Underwater imaging
conditions, including illumination variations, suspended particles, and water turbidity, significantly
degrade image quality. Furthermore, during long-term evolution, many marine organisms have
developed body colors and textures that are highly similar to their surrounding environments,
enabling effective visual camouflage. This strong integration with the background weakens target
semantic representations and increases the difficulty of accurate segmentation.

To address these challenges, several segmentation methods for underwater marine animals have
been proposed in recent years. Li et al. developed ECDNet [12], which employs cascaded decoding
to progressively refine marine animal predictions. Chen et al. proposed the WaterSNet framework,
which integrates a Siamese-based architecture with Random Style Adaptation (RSA) and feature
fusion strategies to reduce the interference caused by underwater degradation factors, achieving
robust segmentation of camouflaged marine animals [13]. Yan et al. (2024) presented the MAS-
SAM framework, which incorporates adapters, a Hypermap Extraction Module (HEM), and a
Progressive Prediction Decoder (PPD) with attention mechanisms into SAM to improve fine-

=== PDF PAGE 4 ===
grained segmentation of underwater marine animals [14].

With the development of vision foundation models, SAM-based methods have also been explored
for marine animal segmentation. Segment Anything Model 2 UNet (SAM2-UNet) [5] proposed by
Xiong et al. combines the powerful representation capability of SAM2 with a U-shaped decoder. It
achieves competitive performance on MAS3K and RMAS datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness
of SAM2-based architectures for marine animal segmentation. Simultaneously, some studies have
proposed various underwater image enhancement algorithms [15-17] aimed at improving image
quality to provide more favorable inputs for subsequent segmentation tasks.

Although existing methods have achieved promising performance, highly camouflaged marine
animal segmentation remains challenging. The similarity between targets and backgrounds weakens
semantic representations, while complex biological structures lead to ambiguous boundaries.
Therefore, further exploration of effective feature refinement and structural guidance strategies is
still required.

2.2 Shape Prior Guided Segmentation

Recently, shape priors have been introduced into image segmentation tasks to utilize geometric
information and improve structural consistency. Different from purely data-driven methods, shape
priors provide additional structural constraints, allowing models to consider not only region
prediction but also object shape characteristics. Therefore, shape-prior guided methods have great
potential in boundary recovery and complex structure segmentation.

Traditional shape prior methods usually rely on manually designed geometric models or predefined
shape constraints, which may have limited flexibility when handling objects with large shape
variations. To better integrate shape constraints into modern deep segmentation networks, Zhao et
al. proposed the Convex Combination Star Shape (CCS) prior for data-driven image semantic
segmentation at CVPR 2025 [22]. The proposed method describes object geometry through a
differentiable shape field and introduces star-shape constraints into the segmentation process.
Compared with traditional shape priors, CCS provides a more flexible data-driven strategy for
preserving object structures while maintaining segmentation accuracy.

However, directly applying CCS to camouflaged marine animal segmentation remains challenging.
The effectiveness of shape refinement depends on the  reliability of input features. Due to
camouflage characteristics, target semantics can be weakened and background interference can be
introduced, which limits the effectiveness of shape priors. Therefore, improving feature reliability
during decoding is essential to fully exploit the advantages of CCS in complex marine scenes.

segmentation frameworks that mainly recover object regions based on visual features, SPMNet

introduces a shape-prior guided progressive decoding strategy to simultaneously restore semantic

SPMNet adopts the frozen pre-trained SAM2 Hiera encoder to exploit  its powerful visual
representation capability. To improve the adaptability of SAM2 features in complex underwater
scenarios, MonaAdapter is introduced for lightweight feature adaptation, while the CFEM and RFB
further enhance target-related responses and multi-scale contextual information. During decoding,
the proposed PSD serves as the core decoding architecture and progressively reconstructs target
structures through spatial feature refinement and shape-prior guidance. Specifically, the SAG
suppresses camouflage-induced background interference during cross-level feature fusion and
improves feature  reliability for subsequent shape-guided decoding. Based on the enhanced
representations, the ICCS mechanism incorporates the CCS prior into the decoding process, where
the shape field provides explicit geometric constraints to refine ambiguous boundaries and slender
structures.  Finally, HSAL  jointly  optimizes  region-level segmentation accuracy and shape
consistency, encouraging the network to generate accurate and structurally coherent segmentation
results.

3.2 Feature Adaptation and Enhancement

Although the SAM2 Hiera encoder provides powerful general visual representations, the domain
gap between its pre-training data and camouflaged marine animal segmentation may limit its
adaptability to complex underwater scenarios. Therefore, based on the frozen SAM2 Hiera
backbone, we introduce feature adaptation and enhancement strategies to improve the transferability
of SAM2 features while preserving the rich visual priors learned from large-scale pre-training.

3.2.1 Lightweight Feature Adaptation with MonaAdapter

MonaAdapter performs lightweight feature adaptation through a bottleneck transformation and
residual fusion. This introduces task-specific information while keeping the SAM2 backbone frozen.

Specifically, given the input feature 𝐹𝑖𝑛 of the 𝑙-th Transformer encoder block, MonaAdapter first
projects the high-dimensional feature into a low-dimensional space through the projection matrix
𝑊𝑑𝑜𝑤𝑛. The feature is then processed by a nonlinear transformation function 𝜙(∙) and restored to
the original dimension through 𝑊𝑢𝑝. Finally, the adapted feature is fused with the original input
through a residual connection, which can be formulated as:


                      𝐹𝑜𝑢𝑡= 𝐹𝑖𝑛+ 𝑊𝑢𝑝(𝜙(𝑊𝑑𝑜𝑤𝑛(𝐹𝑖𝑛)))                        (1)

Where 𝑊𝑑𝑜𝑤𝑛  and 𝑊𝑢𝑝  represent  the  learnable parameters  for  feature compression and
restoration,  respectively, and  𝜙(∙)  denotes  the  nonlinear mapping  function. Through  this
parameter-efficient   adaptation    strategy,   MonaAdapter   introduces   underwater-specific
representations while maintaining the original knowledge of SAM2.

3.2.2 Contextual Feature Enhancement with CFEM and RFB

Although the SAM2 features adapted by MonaAdapter provide strong semantic representations,
camouflaged marine animals usually contain low-contrast regions, weak responses, and complex
non-rigid structures. Therefore, relying only on encoder outputs may result in insufficient local
details. To further improve feature representation, we employ the CFEM and RFB. Given the input
feature 𝐹, CFEM enhances target-related responses by jointly modeling channel-wise and spatial-
wise information. First, the channel-enhanced feature is obtained through channel attention:

                           𝐹𝑐= 𝐹⊗𝑀𝑐                                  (2)

where 𝑀𝑐 denotes the channel attention weight and ⊗ represents element-wise multiplication.
Subsequently, spatial attention is applied to recalibrate spatial responses:

                            𝐹𝑠= 𝐹𝑐⊗𝑀𝑠                                  (3)

where 𝑀𝑠 represents  the  spatial  attention  weight. Through  this  process, CFEM  enhances
discriminative target features while suppressing redundant background information caused by
camouflage.

RFB is further employed to complement multi-scale contextual information[8]. As illustrated in Fig.
2, its convolutional branches capture spatial information at different scales, helping strengthen weak
target representations. This design enlarges the feature perception range and enables the network to
exploit contextual dependencies for recovering weak target responses. The enhanced features
generated by CFEM and RFB are subsequently fed into the Progressive SAG-CCS Decoder (PSD),
providing more reliable representations for shape-prior guided progressive decoding.

3.3 Progressive SAG-CCS Decoder

Although the aforementioned feature adaptation and enhancement strategies improve SAM2 feature
representations,  conventional  vision-driven  decoders  remain  limited  in  handling  semantic
weakening and boundary ambiguity in marine animal segmentation. The high similarity between
targets and backgrounds causes shallow features from cross-level fusion to introduce redundant
background interference, while the lack of explicit geometric constraints limits boundary recovery
for low-contrast regions and slender structures such as tentacles and fins.

Based on these considerations, PSD performs progressive decoding with explicit shape guidance.
PSD first incorporates the ICCS mechanism, which introduces the CCS prior into the decoding
process. By leveraging the shape field as an explicit geometric constraint, ICCS enhances structural
continuity and boundary completeness. However, the effectiveness of shape-prior optimization
depends on reliable feature representations, which can be degraded by camouflage-induced
interference. Therefore, PSD further introduces the SAG to enhance target-related responses and
suppress background interference during feature fusion, providing reliable representations for ICCS.
Through the collaboration between ICCS and SAG, PSD achieves progressive shape-guided
structural reconstruction.

3.3.1 Iterative CCS Refinement

To further address boundary ambiguity and structural incompleteness in camouflaged marine animal
segmentation, we introduce the ICCS mechanism based on the CCS prior into the PSD framework.
Due to the low contrast, complex contours, and irregular structures of camouflaged objects, pixel-
wise prediction based solely on learned visual representations often suffers from insufficient
boundary responses and discontinuous slender structures. Compared with approaches relying only
on local appearance information, shape priors provide structural constraints at the object level,
enabling predictions that better conform to the intrinsic geometry of the targets.

The CCS prior is built upon the star-shape constraint, which characterizes geometric consistency
through the convex combination relationship between an interior center point and boundary points.

any boundary point 𝑥∈𝜕Ω remains inside the object region, the region satisfies the star-shape
constraint:

where 𝑐 denotes the star center, 𝑥 represents a boundary point, and 𝜆  is the convex combination

3.3.2 SAG-guided Progressive Feature Reconstruction

Although ICCS introduces the CCS shape prior to constrain structural refinement, its effectiveness
relies on the reliability of the decoded representations. In camouflaged marine animal segmentation,
the high similarity between targets and backgrounds may introduce irrelevant responses during
cross-level feature fusion. Meanwhile, shallow features contain detailed spatial information but may
also include background interference, which affects the representation of target regions.

To improve the reliability of decoding features, PSD introduces the SAG to adjust spatial responses
during progressive feature reconstruction. The main purpose of SAG is to enhance target-related
regions while suppressing background interference, providing more reliable representations for the
subsequent Seg Head and Shape Head. SAG generates spatial weights to adaptively refine local
feature responses under camouflage conditions.

Given an input decoding feature  𝐹𝑖 , SAG first reduces the feature dimension through a 1×1
convolution and then generates a single-channel spatial attention map using a 7×7 convolution:

                                              𝐹𝑖′ = 𝐹𝑖⨀𝑀𝑖                                (12)

camouflaged  objects.  Therefore, we propose  the HSAL, which  jointly  considers  regional

4. Experiments

4.1 Implementation Details

We employ the Hiera-L version of SAM2 as the frozen backbone of SPMNet. During training, all
images and masks are resized to 352 × 352 pixels. AdamW is used with an initial learning rate of
0.001, a weight decay of 0.0001, and a batch size of 16. Experiments are performed with Python
3.10 and PyTorch 2.6.0 on a single 24-GB NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

4.2 Data Sets

In this study, we evaluate SPMNet on four widely used marine animal segmentation benchmark
datasets, including RMAS, MAS3K, UFO120, and RUWI. RMAS (Real-world Marine Animal
Segmentation) contains more than 2,000 real-world marine scene images and focuses on challenging
underwater conditions, including illumination variation, contrast changes, object occlusion, and
background  interference  [19]. MAS3K  (Marine Animal  Segmentation  3000)  consists  of
approximately 3,000 high-resolution underwater images with pixel-level annotations, containing
complex backgrounds, diverse object scales, and significant target appearance variations [18].
UFO120 (Underwater Fish and Object dataset 120) contains 1,620 samples, including 1,500 images
for training/validation and 120 images for testing, and provides a challenging environment with low
contrast, scattering effects, and color distortion [23]. RUWI (Realistic Underwater Images) focuses
on realistic underwater scenarios with diverse illumination conditions, turbid backgrounds, and
object occlusions, together with  pixel-level annotations  [24]. These  datasets cover various
challenges  in camouflaged marine animal segmentation, including background  interference,
appearance  similarity, and structural complexity, providing a comprehensive benchmark for
evaluating SPMNet.

4.3 Evaluation Metrics

Five metrics are used for evaluation: mean Intersection over Union (𝑚𝐼𝑜𝑈), Structure Measure (𝑆∝),
Weighted F-measure (𝐹𝛽𝜔 ), Mean Enhanced Alignment Measure (𝑚𝐸𝜑 ), Mean Absolute Error
(MAE). These metrics provide a comprehensive evaluation of segmentation performance from
different aspects.

The improvements mainly benefit from the proposed shape-prior guided progressive decoding

=== PDF PAGE 14 ===
strategy. By combining SAG and ICCS, PSD enhances the reliability of decoding features and
preserves the  structural information of camouflaged  targets.  Specifically, SAG reduces the
interference from camouflage backgrounds during cross-level feature fusion, providing more
reliable representations for subsequent refinement. Then, ICCS introduces explicit geometric
constraints through the CCS shape prior and progressively corrects the predictions through iterative
refinement, which improves the recovery of ambiguous boundaries and slender structures. In
addition, HSAL further constrains the consistency between predicted regions and target geometry,
allowing SPMNet to obtain more accurate segmentation results.

different feature levels.

The heatmaps indicate that the conventional decoder still produces noticeable responses from
background regions at different feature levels. This issue is more obvious when the target shares
similar appearances with the surrounding environment, where complex textures may introduce
interference. At the shallow feature stage (a), PSD reduces irrelevant background activations and
produces more concentrated responses on target regions. In the middle feature stages (b) and (c),
the responses of the conventional decoder are relatively scattered, and some target details are

affected by background information, while PSD enhances target-related responses and preserves
more local structural information. At the deep feature stage  (d), PSD further improves the
completeness of target-related responses, making the activated regions more consistent with the
object structure. These observations suggest that PSD can reduce background interference and
enhance target-related feature representations across different feature levels.

4.5.2 Visualization of ICCS Iterative Optimization

To further analyze the influence of ICCS at different iteration stages, we visualize the segmentation
results with different iteration numbers. As shown in Fig. 6, a local region with complex boundary
structures is selected and enlarged for detailed comparison, where T denotes the number of ICCS
iterations. This experiment aims to provide an intuitive observation of how the prediction quality
changes in terms of structural completeness and boundary accuracy during the iterative process.





                                       Fig. 6 ICCS iterative refinement.

The enlarged local regions show the progressive changes of prediction results with T=1, 5, 10, and
15 iterations. The results at different iteration stages demonstrate that ICCS progressively reduces
structural errors in the initial prediction. With a small number of iterations (T=1), the model can
roughly locate the target region, but irregular boundaries remain and deviate from the GT. When T
increases to 5, the object contour becomes clearer and more structural details are recovered. At T=10,
the prediction achieves better consistency with the GT and provides a favorable balance between
boundary continuity and structural completeness. Further increasing the iteration number to T=15
does not bring additional improvements and even introduces slight over-smoothing in local regions.
Therefore, T=10 is adopted as the default iteration number in this work.

4.5.3 Effectiveness of HSAL

Since PSD introduces structural constraints based on the CCS shape prior, the original IoU-BCE
loss, which mainly focuses on region overlap and pixel classification, cannot fully supervise the
geometric consistency of the predictions. Therefore, we design the Hybrid Shape-Aware Loss

=== PDF PAGE 18 ===
(HSAL) by introducing additional shape-aware constraints. To compare the optimization behavior
of the two loss functions, their training curves are normalized by their respective initial loss values,
as shown in Fig. 7.





             Fig. 7 Normalized training loss comparison between Conventional Loss and HSAL.

The normalized curves show that both loss functions decrease rapidly at the beginning of training,
while HSAL exhibits a more pronounced decline and reaches a lower relative loss range earlier. As
training progresses, HSAL shows several noticeable local fluctuations in the middle stage,
indicating more complex optimization dynamics after introducing the shape-aware constraint.
Nevertheless, these fluctuations do not interrupt the overall decreasing trend, and the loss continues
to decline afterward. In comparison, the Conventional IoU-BCE Loss changes more smoothly but
decreases at a slower rate. During the later stage, the fluctuations of HSAL gradually become smaller
and the curve eventually stabilizes. Overall, the advantage of HSAL does not lie in producing a
smoother training process. Instead, it maintains effective optimization under additional structural
supervision and reaches a stable state in the later stage. This suggests that HSAL provides
supervision that is better aligned with the shape-guided decoding process of PSD, allowing regional
prediction and structural constraints to be optimized jointly.

5. Conclusion

In this work, we propose SPMNet to address strong target-background similarity and ambiguous
boundaries in marine animal segmentation. Built on SAM2, SPMNet improves underwater feature
representation through feature adaptation and enhancement, and introduces a PSD for shape-prior

=== PDF PAGE 19 ===
guided progressive decoding. SAG reduces background interference, while ICCS uses the CCS
shape prior to iteratively constrain complex structures and ambiguous boundaries. In addition, the
HSAL combines region supervision with shape-aware constraints to better align the training
objective with the decoding process. Experiments on MAS3K, RMAS, UFO120, and RUWI
demonstrate competitive performance across multiple metrics, while qualitative and ablation results
further verify the effectiveness of PSD, ICCS, and HSAL. Future work will explore more efficient
iterative strategies and more flexible structural priors to reduce computational overhead and
improve adaptability to complex underwater targets.
